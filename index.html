01

Background

On 25 May 2005, Europe’s two most decorated clubs of the night met in Istanbul. AC Milan arrived as favourites: Carlo Ancelotti’s side had Kaká, Andriy Shevchenko, Paolo Maldini, and a midfield of Andrea Pirlo, Gennaro Gattuso and Clarence Seedorf. Liverpool, managed by Rafael Benítez in his first season, had scraped through a group stage and then beaten Juventus and Chelsea on the way to the final. Few outside the red half of Merseyside expected them to lift a fifth European Cup, 21 years after their last.

The venue was the Atatürk Olympic Stadium, a vast bowl on the city’s outskirts. Liverpool supporters filled the night with “You’ll Never Walk Alone” before kick-off. Spanish referee Manuel Mejuto González took charge. Milan wore white; Liverpool wore red. Harry Kewell started despite fitness doubts. Dietmar Hamann began on the bench.

Road to the final

Milan edged PSV on away goals. Liverpool knocked out Chelsea in a tense semi-final at Anfield.

The favourite

Milan had conceded only six Champions League goals all season and were widely expected to win a seventh European Cup.

02

First Half

The final was decided at one end of the pitch for 45 minutes. After 52 seconds, Kaká’s clipped free-kick found Maldini, who volleyed past Jerzy Dudek for the fastest goal in a Champions League final. Milan were in complete control. Kaká ran the midfield; Shevchenko and Hernán Crespo stretched a Liverpool defence that looked overmatched.

Kewell lasted only 23 minutes before coming off injured, replaced by Vladimír Šmicer. The damage grew late in the half. In the 39th minute Kaká slipped Crespo through and the Argentine finished. Five minutes later Shevchenko’s clever pass set Crespo free again for a dink over Dudek. At half-time it was 3–0. Liverpool fans sat in silence. Television commentators spoke as if the trophy had already left for Milan.

03

Second Half

Benítez changed shape at the interval. Steve Finnan made way for Hamann, and Liverpool switched to a 3-5-2. The idea was simple: crowd Kaká, give Steven Gerrard licence to attack, and refuse to accept the night was over. Six minutes into the half, John Arne Riise whipped in a cross and Gerrard headed in. The stadium shifted.

Two minutes later Šmicer, playing his last match for the club, struck from outside the box. Dida could only help the ball in. 3–2. On the hour, Gattuso fouled Gerrard in the area. Xabi Alonso’s penalty was saved, but Alonso smashed in the rebound. In six minutes Liverpool had erased a three-goal deficit. The Kop in exile found its voice again. Milan, stunned, could not recapture the first-half rhythm. Djimi Traoré later cleared a Shevchenko effort off the line after Dudek spilled a cross. Full time: 3–3.

04

Additional Time

Extra time belonged to Milan’s pressure and Liverpool’s resistance. Legs cramped. Jamie Carragher played through exhaustion. Benítez introduced Djibril Cissé; Ancelotti turned to Jon Dahl Tomasson, Serginho and later Rui Costa. Liverpool sat deeper. Gerrard dropped into a more protective role. Milan kept the ball and hunted a fourth.

With about two minutes left, Shevchenko met a cross at point-blank range. Dudek blocked the header, then somehow clawed away the follow-up from a yard. It was the save that made the penalties possible — a moment still replayed whenever the night is retold. The referee ended extra time at 3–3. For the second time in three years, a Champions League final would be settled from the spot. In 2003, Shevchenko had won one for Milan. In Istanbul, he would have to take another.

05

Penalties

The shoot-out was taken at the Liverpool end. Dudek copied Bruce Grobbelaar’s “spaghetti legs” from 1984, bouncing on the line to unsettle the kickers. Serginho blasted Milan’s first penalty over. Hamann scored. Dudek saved Pirlo’s low kick. Cissé scored. Tomasson pulled Milan back. Riise’s effort was saved by Dida. Kaká scored. Šmicer, who had already written himself into the comeback, sent Dida the wrong way to make it 3–2. Shevchenko had to score. Dudek saved to his left. Liverpool were European champions.

06

Aftermath

Gerrard lifted the trophy. Liverpool became the fifth club awarded the European Cup to keep permanently, joining Real Madrid, Milan, Bayern Munich and Ajax. It was their first European title since Rome in 1984, and Benítez’s defining night. Dudek, who would soon lose his place to Pepe Reina, was the unlikely hero. Šmicer left the club having scored in a European Cup final and converted a decisive penalty.

The match entered football language as the Miracle of Istanbul. Milan were left to replay a collapse that still haunts a golden generation; they would meet Liverpool again in Athens in 2007 and win 2–1. For Liverpool supporters, 25 May 2005 became a civic holiday: an open-top parade, a city in red, and a story told to every new generation — three-nil down, never beaten, never walking alone.
